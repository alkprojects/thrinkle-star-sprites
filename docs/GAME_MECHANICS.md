# GAME_MECHANICS.md — Twinkle Star Sprites (ADK, Neo Geo MVS, 1996)

**Purpose:** Definitive mechanics reference for a faithful web reimplementation. This document records the ORIGINAL 2-player arcade game only. All claims were adversarially verified against primary sources; where sources conflict, both readings are given and the more credible one is identified. Anything refuted, contradicted, or unverifiable is listed in §13.

**Canonical version:** Neo Geo MVS arcade ROM (`twinspri`), including its slowdown behavior (§11).

---

## 1. Overview

Twinkle Star Sprites is a **competitive ("versus") shoot-'em-up**: two players play simultaneously on two side-by-side vertical playfields on one screen, each confined to their own field. You can never directly enter or fire into the opponent's field — *"You can't directly fire at each other, but instead you attack indirectly by creating combos by shooting the enemies that come down your way"* (HG101).

The core loop:

1. **Waves of harmless "zako" (popcorn) enemies** fly fixed paths across your field. They never shoot; their only threat is collision.
2. Destroying a zako creates an **explosion**, which detonates overlapping zako and incoming enemy fireballs — a **chain (combo, 連爆)**.
3. Chains of 4+ hits **send fireball attacks onto the opponent's field**. Bigger chains send more, bigger, tougher fireballs.
4. The opponent can **dodge** your fireballs or **shoot them down**, which *reflects* them back at you as flashing **Reverse Attacks**. Reflecting reverses escalates further into indestructible **Extra Attacks** and ultimately **Boss Attacks** (§4) — an escalating volley the 1996 developers tuned "like playing beanbag or hackeysack."
5. A separate **charge meter** path lets players spend banked meter on Extra Attacks (Level 2) or a Boss (MAX).
6. Each player has **5 hearts**. Opponent attacks cost 3 hearts; own-field zako collisions cost 1 (and can never kill). Every time you take damage, **your opponent heals** (§5) — a zero-sum life economy.
7. A round ends when a player hits 0 hearts or touches **Death**, the anti-stall reaper who spawns ~100 seconds in. Matches are **best 2-of-3 rounds**, fighting-game style.

Periodic **Fever orbs**, **Perfect** bonuses for one-shot wave clears, bombs, and a 13-character roster with asymmetric stats layer on top. ADK internally called it the "juice game" because matches resolve fast enough to settle who fetches drinks.

## 2. Playfield, Controls & Movement

### 2.1 Playfield layout

- One screen, split into two vertical playfields. **P1 = left half, P2 = right half.** Each player is permanently confined to their own field.
- Community wall terminology (origin: Mike Uyama's 2009 GameFAQs Versus Guide — *not* Mizuumi, whose Glossary page does not exist):
  - **Outer wall** = the screen edge (P1's left, P2's right).
  - **Inner wall** = the central divider (P1's right, P2's left).
- All **attack objects** (fireballs, reverse attacks, extra attacks, bosses) are generated by actions on your field and delivered onto the opponent's field. However, **not all cross-player interaction is mediated by objects** — three non-object channels exist:
  1. **Life transfer:** when you take damage, your opponent heals (§5.3).
  2. **Perfect tempo effects:** a Perfect speeds up the attacks you send, slows attacks sent to you, and modifies formation pacing on the opponent's screen (§10.4) — a direct cross-field effect with nothing crossing the screen.
  3. **Boss clash/displacement:** near-simultaneous bosses cancel; a new boss can displace an existing one (§4.5).

### 2.2 HUD (per player)

- **Score** at top, **hearts directly below it** (5 hearts at round start, tracked in half-heart increments).
- **Charge gauge** at bottom (bottom-left corner for P1, bottom-right for P2): a clear gauge with a red fill bar, partitioned into three marked sections — **1 / 2 / MAX**.
- **Bomb stock** as circled "B" icons at the bottom of your side. Display caps at 4; actual stock is unlimited (*"The game only displays up to four bombs, but it's possible to hold more"* — mikwuyma).
- There is **no visible round timer**; the ~100-second Death rule is hidden.

### 2.3 Controls

- **8-way joystick** (diagonals included) + exactly **two gameplay buttons**:
  - **A** — tap to shoot; **hold to charge**, release to fire the charge attack (official in-game term: **"Attack Stopper"**).
  - **B** — bomb (fires on press).
- C and D are unused. One character-specific wrinkle: Macky & Pentell's Attack Stopper projectile is detonated by pressing **A a second time** — still the A button, not a third button.
- **Shot limit:** at most **2 of your own shots on screen at once** (mikwuyma: *"You can only have two shots on the screen at once"*). Effective fire rate is therefore governed by shot travel speed (§9.2). ⚠ Single-sourced; treat the exact number 2 as medium confidence.

### 2.4 Movement

- **Diagonal movement is ~1.4× faster than cardinal** (consistent with √2 ≈ 1.414, though the source wording is "a little over 1.4 times") because the horizontal and vertical velocity components are kept at full cardinal speed when combined — **the velocity vector is not normalized**. Characters cross the screen in the same number of frames whether moving diagonally or cardinally (per-axis components are independent). A faithful port must NOT normalize diagonal velocity.
- *"Diagonal movement won't help you outrun things you couldn't outrun before"* — it's the same per-axis speed.
- Wall-to-wall traversal times (60 fps, lag-free; full table in §8/§9): horizontal 36f (Schmitt, fastest) to 69f (Yan Yanyung, slowest); vertical 52f to 99f. **Horizontal crossing takes ~70% of vertical crossing time**, fixing relative field proportions.
- Positional meta (Mizuumi Strategy): safe pockets are the **upper-outer wall** and **lower-inner wall**; the **bottom-outer corner** is the worst spot under pressure; "moving down is nearly always safe" because parabolic/diagonal fireballs usually enter from the upper-inside corner. Mirrored for P2.

### 2.5 Hitboxes (engine model)

- Collision is **center-point + per-sprite-frame offset AABB**: each object stores x,y and looks up four offsets (X1, X2, Y1, Y2) from a ROM offset library, giving a box spanning [x−X1, x+X2] × [y−Y1, y+Y2]. Offsets are per animation frame (Schmitt's EX rocket cycles three hitboxes in flight). Asymmetric extents are required (source: community Trainer ROM reverse engineering; medium confidence).
- Hit detection: player box vs every sprite, Y-overlap rejected first; any overlap = hit.
- **All hurtboxes are smaller than sprites** — characters are shorter and/or skinnier than drawn.
- **All characters share the exact same shot hitbox**, including while dizzy (the dizzy shot looks smaller and is weaker, but the collision box is unchanged — Trainer ROM finding, medium confidence).
- Known genuine bug: mirrored-sprite hitboxes swap the X offsets instead of mirroring them, producing displaced/inverted hitboxes — manifests on the right-side lightning attack ("the lightning sometimes doesn't kill you"). A faithful port must decide to reproduce or fix (§13).

## 3. Enemy Waves & the Chain System

### 3.1 Zako (popcorn enemies)

- Arrive in **exactly 36 fixed formations** (numbered 0–35 in Mizuumi's chart; community-grouped as 12 Simple / 10 Intermediate / 4 Bubble / 10 Advanced). All fly predetermined, memorizable paths and exit with **no penalty** if undestroyed (deliberately letting waves or the fever orb pass is a legitimate tactic — オーブ見逃し in JP score play).
- Formations enter from the top, except **one unique "back-attack" formation** that enters from the bottom corners.
- **Zako never fire projectiles.** Their only threat is collision (1 heart, stun — §5).
- **Durability is encoded by color:** Purple/Pink = 5 hits, Blue = 4, Green = 3, Yellow = 2, Red = 1. Each 1-damage shot drops the zako one color tier; multi-damage hits skip tiers (a 3-damage hit turns a blue zako directly red). Color is a direct readout of remaining HP.
- **Explosion size scales with the zako's sprite size** (not its current color); large zako (usually the purple tier) make big blasts that destroy smaller zako regardless of their color. The debug ZAKO menu exposes a "Big" size parameter per wave.
- A charge shot dealing ≥5 instant damage one-shots the toughest zako; Yan's 4-damage wave fails against durability-5 zako (they pierce through it).
- **Bubbled (shabon) zako:** some zako arrive in bubbles. One shot (normal or charge) pops the bubble; the inner zako is invulnerable during the ~0.5 s pop animation. An explosion touching a bubbled zako pops the bubble but does NOT harm the zako inside (bubbles break chains — pre-pop them for Perfects). If every zako in an upcoming formation would be bubbled, a "!!" warning fires and a **star coin** appears; caught star-side-up it pops every bubble on your screen. Bubbled zako **never appear while you have Fever**.
- **Pacing:** zako/formations get a global speed-up around **100 in-game seconds** into a round (about when Death appears). Per one source (namu.wiki, medium confidence) the next formation does not spawn until the current one has fully left the screen, so fast clears accelerate the cycle. Formations can overlap in practice via lingering explosions.
- **Formation selection:** the next formation is drawn unpredictably (RNG) from a pool that starts as the 12 Simple formations and expands over round time; the back-attack formation has a long re-appearance cooldown. The **left/right mirroring of formations strictly alternates** (not random; single expert source). Within a few formations a sub-group picks a random entry side independent of the global alternation. There is suggestive (unconfirmed) evidence both players receive the same formation sequence, progressing at different speeds. Several formations are **position-reactive**: they aim their layout at your position on spawn or scatter/strafe at your height/column.

### 3.2 The chain (combo) system

- A combo is a **chain reaction of explosions**: destroying one zako creates an explosion that detonates overlapping/nearby zako AND incoming fireballs; each object caught counts as **+1 hit** and explodes in turn.
- **Explosions are first-class collision objects** with persistent hitboxes — model them as timed area entities, not instant radius queries. Their active frames **outlive the visible animation** (Ran's EX explosion stays lethal ~0.5 s, "even if it looks like the explosion has mostly disappeared"). No exact zako-explosion lifetime is published; ~30 active frames at 60 fps is a reasonable tunable starting value (§13).
- **There is no documented combo timer** — chaining is purely spatial contagion. Explosions persist long enough to catch newly arriving formations (a combo can bridge formations), and JP score play "reignites" chains by holding rapid fire near the top of the screen as the next formation spawns.
- One explosion can detonate any number of overlapping objects; there is no per-explosion target cap.
- Opponent fireballs caught in your explosions are **reflected AND count as combo hits** (this is how chains are extended during fever).
- The in-match counter displays up to **32 hits**, then shows **"MAX"** for each further hit; the round-results screen tracks up to **99**.

### 3.3 Chain size → attacks sent (the core mapping)

**Normal (non-fever) mode:** the 4th hit sends 1 fireball, and every 2 hits after that sends 1 more.

> **fireballs = floor((hits − 2) / 2)**, for combos of 4+ hits

| Combo hits | Fireballs sent (normal) | Fireballs sent (fever: hits − 1) |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1–3 | 0 | 0–2 |
| 4 | 1 | 3 |
| 5 | 1 | 4 |
| 6 | 2 | 5 |
| 7 | 2 | 6 |
| 8 | 3 | 7 |
| 9 | 3 | 8 |
| 10 | 4 | 9 |
| 12 | 5 | 11 |
| 16 | 7 | 15 |
| 20 | 9 | 19 |
| 32 | 15 | 31 |

(Confirmed independently by mikwuyma's formula and the Japanese Niconico Daihyakka: 4連爆=1体, 6=2, 8=3, +1 per 2 thereafter.)

**Fireball size/HP scales with the hit index that generated it:**

| Combo hit # that generated it | Fireball size | Shots to destroy/reflect |
|---|---|---:|
| 2nd–5th | small | 2 |
| 6th–10th | medium | 3 |
| 11th–15th | big | 4 |
| 16th+ | biggest | 5 |

The table starts at hit 2 because in fever mode generation starts from the 2nd hit; in normal mode it starts at hit 4 (so in normal mode the small tier only ever comes from hits 4–5). Big combos send not only more but tougher-to-reflect fireballs.

- Fireballs are **dispatched sequentially as the triggering hits occur** during chain propagation (Mizuumi: the pattern can change "mid combo") — a long-combo volley arrives staggered at the chain's rhythm, not as one simultaneous wall.
- Fireballs are color-coded by sender: **orange = P1, purple = P2** (mikwuyma; KLinsenbardt and HG101 call P2's "blue" — same bluish-purple sprite).
- Documented anomaly: *"Occasionally a combo will create an extra attack in place of a couple of fireballs. There is no known explanation for this phenomenon."* (mikwuyma, single source.)

### 3.4 Fireball flight patterns on the receiving field

Three patterns, cycling in a **fixed order** (Mizuumi, sole source for the cycling rule):

1. **Parabola** (arc; can arc over the top into corners),
2. **Diagonal Bounce** (enters at a downward angle through the **inner wall**, heads straight for the victim's current position; **faster** than straight fireballs),
3. **Stop-and-wait at top** ("straight" fireballs: fly to the top, hover while tracking the victim's horizontal position, then fall straight down).

The starting pattern may be random, but the cycle order is fixed; **the sender's pattern advances when the sender's formation changes — even mid-combo** (pattern is a property of the sender's current formation, not of each fireball). Both aimed types use a **snapshot aim** (no continuous homing) and can be baited: sit where you want them aimed, then move late. A fireball that crosses the field without hitting simply exits and despawns — no looping, wrapping, or returning.

## 4. The Attack Escalation Ladder

### 4.1 The four tiers

| Tier | What it is | Can be destroyed? | Can be reflected/escalated? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Normal Attack** (fireball) | Generic fireball, orange (P1) / purple-blue (P2), sized 2–5 HP by combo depth | Yes — shoot it (2–5 hits by size) or catch it in a chain explosion | Yes → becomes a Reverse Attack on the original sender's field |
| **Reverse Attack** | The reflected fireball, flashing green/purple, **slightly faster** than a normal | Yes — by direct shots or explosions | Yes → 1 destroyed individually = 1 Extra Attack; **3+ destroyed by explosions within one combo = Boss instead** |
| **Extra Attack** | Character-specific indestructible creature/projectile | **No** — absorbs shots; bombs do NOT erase it (bomb i-frames just let you survive it) | **No — ladder terminates here.** Dodge only |
| **Boss Attack** | The sender's character-specific boss on the victim's field | Yes — 17–25 HP, killable by shots/charge/bombs (center hitbox only; zako explosions never damage it) | Not reflectable, but displaceable via the reversal-boss mechanic |

Key clarifications (these correct common misstatements):

- **Reflection of a normal fireball** works by direct shots OR explosions; both return it to the sender's field as a reverse.
- **Reverse → Extra is 1:1 only when reverses are destroyed individually.** If **3 or more reverse attacks are destroyed inside a single combo's explosions, they become one Boss Attack INSTEAD of converting to extras** — the boss replaces them; do not spawn 3 extras plus a boss.
- The **boss trigger is explosion-only**: *"Reflecting reverse attacks with a direct attack will not work, the reverse attacks need to be reflected by an explosion. However, three separate enemy explosions can trigger a boss attack, as long as the explosions are part of the same combo"* (mikwuyma). Direct-shot reflections of reverses still each send an extra but never count toward the 3.
- **Fever-yellow flashing fireballs are NOT reverse attacks** — they are normal-class attacks that happen to flash yellow (classic beginner confusion). Strategy rule: *"Reflect fireballs that flash green or purple, but don't reflect fireballs that don't flash or flash yellow."*
- There is **no infinitely escalating bounce loop**: the only speed bump in the ladder is the single Normal→Reverse step. Escalation past Reverse is by **count** (3 reverses in one chain = boss) or by **meter**, never by repeated bouncing.
- Edge case from JP expert play (503sprites): a fireball destroyed by **shots** returns as a reverse **one size LARGER** than the fireball shot down, while one destroyed inside a **chain explosion** returns at the size corresponding to that chain's hit count (a max-size fireball caught in a 2-chain returns as the smallest reverse).
- Core strategy consequence: **dodge normal attacks** (reflecting them arms the opponent with reverses) but **reflect reverse attacks**. Defensive rule when blowing up mixed clusters (note: an earlier research pass had this inverted — this is the correct source wording): *"If there is a cluster of reverse attacks and normal attacks, make sure there are less than three NORMAL attacks in the cluster before reflecting, or else it's likely you will receive a boss attack"* — exploding 3+ of the opponent's plain fireballs hands them reverses they can chain into a boss against you.

### 4.2 Meter path (parallel to the reflection path)

- **Level 2 charge release** → fires your charge shot AND sends **2–4 character-specific Extra Attacks** (counts in §8).
- **MAX charge release** → fires your charge shot AND sends a **Boss**.
- **MAX release while a boss is already on the opponent's screen** → no second boss; instead you send your Level-2 extra count **+2** (mikwuyma: Schmitt 4+2=6 rockets, Nanja 2+2=4 furballs). ASaoshi instead describes it as "double" with an explicit exception: Tinker & Linker send only **4** (not 5 per +2, not 6 per double). Conflict unresolved — verify per character in emulation (§13). Mizuumi corroborates the mechanic: "If you have a boss on the screen, lv 3 meter will send more EXs at a time."

### 4.4 Boss triggers (summary)

A boss is sent to the opponent exactly two ways:

1. **MAX-level charge release** (requires a full charge bar).
2. **Reflect 3 reverse attacks via explosion(s) within a single combo** (one explosion or several, same combo; direct shots never count).

ASaoshi reports an extreme case of "9 Boss Attacks" from a large reverse-catch — mechanism unverified (§13).

### 4.5 Boss clash, reversal, displacement

- **Clash:** if both players trigger bosses at nearly the same time when no boss is yet on screen, the bosses cancel and neither spawns. Window: mikwuyma "within a second of each other"; ASaoshi "simultaneously (or within a few seconds before yours or theirs has appeared)"; Mizuumi "too close to each other." No frame-exact value exists. A plausible (unverified) implementation: the clash window equals the first boss's white-flash spawn period.
- **Reversal boss:** triggering a boss (3-reverse combo OR MAX charge) **while a boss is on YOUR OWN screen** sends that boss away and spawns **the triggering player's own character boss** on the opponent's screen (Mizuumi: "their boss will leave your screen and your boss shows up on theirs" — it is a new boss, not the same boss relocating). No extra-attack flood is documented for this single-trigger case.
- **Simultaneous trigger with a boss already out:** the boss "simply move[s] to the empty side, and the person who had a boss on their screen will receive a multitude of extra attacks" (mikwuyma — scoped to the both-trigger case; the "multitude" count is undocumented).
- **Source conflict:** mikwuyma says the clash-cancel "only applies if there is no boss currently on the screen"; ASaoshi presents simultaneous-cancel as overriding even the reversal case. Unresolved — verify in MAME (§13).
- **Only one boss exists game-wide at a time** — never stated explicitly as a rule, but every documented interaction enforces it; treat as a correct inference.

### 4.6 Boss on the victim's field

- **Spawn:** top-center, **flashes white for about one second** (figure single-sourced to mikwuyma; the JP source NMT confirms the flash but gives no duration). While flashing it has **no hit detection at all**: shots pass through, the player can overlap it, nothing damages it. Hit detection begins the moment the flashing ends.
- **Spawn-frame exemption:** on the exact frame hit detection begins, the boss takes damage but **cannot be stunned** even by 5+ damage (that frame is treated like attack mode); a single hit in the following wander mode then stuns. This lets heavy charges land twice before the first stun.
- **Vulnerability:** only the boss's **center region** takes damage — edge hits do nothing (no red damage flash = no damage). **Exception:** during Mevious's boss's tail attack, the hurtbox moves from the body to the tip of the tail.
- **Zako explosion blasts never damage bosses.** Only player shots, charge attacks, and bombs do (bomb = 1×13 ticks; Kim's bomb 1×16).
- **Phases:** a fixed three-mode state machine — **wander (浮遊) → attack (攻撃) → wander → escape (逃げ)** (some attacks skip wanders; body-ram attacks start in attack mode immediately for every boss EXCEPT Kim's and Griffon's). Which attack the boss uses is **random**, but each attack's mode pattern and wander durations are **fully deterministic** (NMT explicitly debunks ADK's published claim that boss appearance time is random) — unless a stun shortens it.
- **Boss stun:** triggers when cumulative damage since the last stun (or since spawn) reaches **5+** and a hit lands **during wander mode**. Damage in attack/escape modes never stuns immediately but banks toward an instant stun on the next wander-mode hit. **Stunned bosses are completely invincible (even to bombs)**; stun duration is identical for all bosses (value unmeasured); stun cancels the current wander, forcing the next mode immediately (shortening the visit). Known 100%-reproducible quirk: bombing Macky & Pentell's boss during its first wander stuns it and it then always escapes without attacking.
- **Duration if ignored:** roughly **10–15 seconds** (ASaoshi; KLinsenbardt says 10–20). Deterministic per attack pattern — implement per-attack scripted timelines, not one global timer.
- **Escape:** most bosses hop up slightly then dive diagonally toward the player's position. Exceptions: Schmitt's rises diagonally toward you briefly then drops straight down; Yan Yanyung's and Mevious's zigzag downward ignoring player position.
- **Boss output:** each boss has its own attack set (2–5 attacks; catalogued per character in §8), can spawn the sender's extra-attack lookalikes (these lookalikes ARE bomb-erasable, unlike real extras — NMT), and all boss projectiles and **boss body contact** deal the standard 3 hearts.
- **Killing a boss instantly erases all of its on-screen attacks.** A boss kill grants the killer meter roughly equal to a Perfect's worth (NMT; the Nico Nico claim that boss kills grant meter is otherwise single-sourced).
- Spawning a boss does **not** clear in-flight fireballs/extras already on the victim's field — everything stacks (this is the boss+EX pressure synergy).
- ASaoshi (single source): "The more times you summon a boss in one round, the stronger it gets" — unverified; NMT lists fixed boss HP (§13).

## 5. Damage & Life Economy

### 5.1 Damage table

Players start each round with **5 hearts** (also the cap; tracked in 0.5 increments). Exactly three things deplete the player's life:

| Damage source | Hearts lost | Side effects | Opponent heals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zako (popcorn) collision | **1** (floor: 0.5 — **cannot kill**; at 1 or 0.5 hearts it leaves you at 0.5) | Mashable stun, then ~5 s dizzy debuff (§5.4) | **+0.5** |
| Any opponent attack: Normal, Reverse, Extra, boss projectile, or boss body contact | **3** | ~1 s invincibility blink (**measured "58ish frames"**) | **+1.0** |
| Death (reaper) contact | **Instant round loss**, regardless of hearts ("no exceptions") | — | nothing (round just ends) |

- With 5 hearts and 3-damage attacks, **two clean attack hits kill from full health** (5 → 2 → dead). At exactly 3 hearts or fewer, **one attack hit kills** (NMT: "３以下まで減ると一発で死んじゃいます").
- The player's own explosions, shots, and the fever orb are harmless to the player.
- Nothing else changes life totals: there is **no passive regeneration and no health pickup item** of any kind. The only in-round healing is the transfer below.

### 5.2 Damage avoidance / i-frames

- After an attack hit: ~58 frames of blinking invincibility (whether it also blocks zako and Death contact is not explicitly stated; standard behavior implies yes — players demonstrably cut through Death during post-hit i-frames per 503sprites).
- During a bomb: invincible from ~1 frame after the button press until the animation/flashing ends.
- Dizzy stun itself grants **no** i-frames: you can be hit by zako **once more** while stunned; after recovering from that second hit you blink with invincibility (max two zako hits "in one go").

### 5.3 Life steal (the core economy)

- **When you take damage, your opponent HEALS: a FIXED amount per hit type, not a fraction of damage.** +0.5 heart per zako collision; **+1.0 heart** per attack hit (not 1.5 — the "opponent recovers half the damage taken" phrasing on EN Wikipedia/SNK Fandom matches the zako case only and is wrong for the attack case; mikwuyma, Mizuumi, NMT's worked doping arithmetic, and JA Wikipedia all agree on the fixed 0.5/1.0).
- Visual: a small heart icon appears for +0.5, a large heart for +1.0 (NamuWiki).
- **Healing is capped at 5 hearts.**
- **Recovery restriction:** once your life drops to **3 hearts or fewer**, passive zako-collision heals can only restore you **up to 3**; to exceed 3 you must land attack hits on the opponent (+1.0 each). (Single-sourced to mikwuyma but behaviorally consistent with NMT's CPU-"doping" lines.)
- Death contact transfers nothing.
- Per-hit swing: an attack hit moves the life differential by 4 (−3/+1); a collision by 1.5 (−1/+0.5). A player can survive unlimited collisions (floor 0.5) but each one hands the opponent half a heart — the basis of the JP "doping" technique for keeping a score-farm CPU alive.
- Undocumented edge case: when a zako hit only costs 0.5 (victim at 1 or 0.5 hearts), no source states whether the opponent's heal is still 0.5 or reduced (§13).

### 5.4 Zako-hit dizzy state

Sequence: collision → **stun** (mashable: mash buttons + wiggle stick to shorten; base duration unpublished) → **~5 seconds** of debuff (figure single-sourced to mikwuyma) → normal.

During the post-stun debuff: reduced movement speed, reduced shot speed, and a smaller/weaker basic shot (Mizuumi: "smaller"; ASaoshi: "less damage" — possibly the same effect). ### 5.5 Bombs

- **Stock:** 2 per round (reset every round; surplus does not carry over). Bomb coins add +1 each; stock is **unlimited** (HUD displays max 4). TV Tropes' "max of 3" is contradicted by mikwuyma and should be ignored.
- **Acquisition:** bomb coins are dropped by a courier zako on a formation-count schedule (first ~50–60 s in; second typically soon after Death appears), preceded by a "!!" warning. Coins flip in flight: caught **bomb-side-up = +1 bomb** ("GET BOMS"); caught dollar-side-up = 10 points (worthless).
- **Effect (precise):**
  - Invincibility from **~1 frame after the press** until the flashing ends (both mikwuyma and NMT note the ~1-frame lag — you can rarely die on the bomb-graphic frame).
  - Coverage is the **player's ENTIRE field regardless of the bomb's graphic**.
  - **Destroys:** all zako (any HP, bubbled or not), Death (one hit, any HP), most boss projectiles. Can detonate the fever orb and can start/extend combos.
  - **Does NOT destroy:** incoming fireballs/attack zako ("攻撃ザコに対しても全く効果がありません" — you survive them via i-frames; they are not cleared), **extra attacks** ("でもエキストラアタックは消えません"), beam-type boss attacks (lasers, lightning), boss body-part attacks (Pentell's punch, Mevious's tail).
  - Your own on-screen shots and charge attacks are erased on activation.
  - **Sends nothing at the opponent.**
  - Boss damage: gradual, totaling **13** (1×13 ticks); Kim's "Great Tsunami" deals **16** (1×16). If the bomb stuns the boss mid-stream, stun invincibility caps realized damage at ~9 (Kim ~12).
  - You cannot shoot or release a charge attack during a bomb, but you CAN keep holding/building charge — and **releasing an Lv2/MAX during a bomb fires nothing and consumes no meter** (meter is spent only when the attack actually fires).
  - Defensive tech: bombing a boss as it spawns its extras/special cancels that emission (e.g., Memory's cake-boss's 6 extras never appear).
  - Character quirk: Load Ran does a flip animation and **cannot move** during her bomb (universally rated the worst bomb); everyone else can move, some with constraints.

### 5.6 Charge meter economy

- The red bar **builds** from chain combos and Perfects (per-zako vs per-hit vs per-combo granularity is unresolved; no numeric amounts published anywhere). NMT adds that killing a boss grants roughly a Perfect's worth of meter.
- The bar is **spent only by Level 2 and MAX releases**. Level 1 charge shots are free and always available. Per Nico Nico (single JP source): Lv2 consumes **one gauge notch**, MAX consumes **two notches** — i.e., a full-bar MAX drops you back to the Level-1 baseline; the L1 segment is never spent.
- Charge level gating is **dual**: hold time determines the level you have charged to (L1 = per-character time, L2 = 2×, MAX = 3× — mikwuyma's explicit multiplier rule), while the red bar gates availability (bar must reach the "2" mark for L2, full for MAX).
- You are vulnerable while charging and cannot fire normal shots.
- **On Neo Geo the meter resets between rounds** (the Saturn port's meter carry-over is explicitly listed as a port difference).
- Mizuumi claims Kim and Realy Till gain meter "incredibly/extremely fast" — no numeric per-character modifier is documented anywhere; treat per-character meter gain as UNCONFIRMED and implement universal gain (§13).

## 6. Fever Mode & Boss Attacks

### 6.1 Fever orb

- A **spinning blue orb** (crescent-moon design) replaces one zako in a formation, after a semi-random number of formations on that player's field (per-player). Observed as early as the **4th formation**, ideal late case ~**12th** (NMT); Wikipedia approximates "30–40 seconds in" (single source). Orbs **recur multiple times** per long round.
- **Perfects make the orb appear sooner** (possibly indirectly via faster formation pacing — §13).
- Telegraph: a "!!" warning pops from the top of the screen before the orb's formation (the same "!!" is also used for coins and all-bubble formations).
- **The orb can ONLY be destroyed by a chain explosion touching it or by a bomb.** Shots and charge attacks have no effect (whether they literally pass through vs harmlessly collide is undocumented). If the orb's formation is not destroyed, it flies away with it — deliberately skippable (this exit detail is uncontradicted but unverified by any consulted source).
- The orb's slot inside a formation is formation-specific (some formations have 2+ possible slots); the trainer ROM's live "countdown to fever orb / star coin formation" display proves the game tracks a concrete formation counter, and the debug "Hoshi" parameter sets the orb's chain position.
- On activation: the game freezes a split second, the announcer yells **"FEVER!"**, and your character + your fireballs flash yellow for the duration.

### 6.2 Fever effects

- **Output formula: every combo hit after the first sends a fireball — (hits − 1) attacks** (9-hit combo → 8 fireballs, vs 3 without fever; even a 2-hit combo sends 1). Versus the normal floor((hits−2)/2), this is **"more than double"** (mikwuyma's own wording): ~2.67× at 9 hits, trending to ~2× for long chains, exceeding 3× only for 4–6-hit combos. (An earlier research pass said "roughly tripling" — wrong.)
- Fever fireballs flash yellow but occupy the **normal-attack** ladder position (reflecting one gives the fevered player a reverse — do not reflect them).
- **Speed conflict:** mikwuyma says fever fireballs "act exactly the same as normal fireballs"; ASaoshi ("fly faster"), TV Tropes ("doubles the speed"), and 1CC Log ("speed... increased considerably") say faster. 3-vs-1 — treat **faster** as the more likely behavior pending frame analysis (§13).
- **Bubbled zako never appear during your fever** (easier chaining).
- JP description adds: zako on your own screen and the attacks you send are sped up substantially during fever.
- Both players can hold fever simultaneously (each field spawns its own orbs); double-fever play has both players feeding each other fireballs to extend combos.

### 6.3 Fever duration

- **Time-limited** — an internal RAM countdown (per the trainer-ROM thread: fever timer at offset $4252,A4 = RAM 0x10C252 P1 / 0x10D252 P2; the bytes at $3668,A4 / 0x10B668 are the "new fever" flag). Fever is active exactly while the timer is nonzero and expires mid-round (NMT's score diary explicitly plans earnings "before fever ends").
- **No source publishes the exact duration** — only "several seconds" / 一定時間. Gameplay-based estimate: on the order of 10–15 s. Must be measured from the ROM for a faithful port (§13).
- ZillionBrain's unconfirmed belief: Perfects may extend fever duration.

### 6.4 Boss attacks (victim-side summary)

Covered in §4.6; per-character boss HP and movesets in §8. One-line recap: spawn top-center with ~1 s intangible white flash → deterministic wander/attack/wander/escape per randomly chosen attack → 3 hearts on any projectile or body contact → killable (center hitbox only, 17–25 HP) → killing it erases all its attacks instantly → leaves on its own in ~10–15 s.

## 7. Rounds, Timer & Win Conditions

- **Versus match: best 2-out-of-3 rounds** ("much like a standard fighting game match"). JA Wikipedia notes 2-points-first is the **standard operator setting**, implying the round count is soft-dip adjustable (alternatives undocumented).
- **Each round resets:** 5 hearts, 2 bombs, charge meter to baseline (Neo Geo). No source documents in-flight attacks carrying across rounds; assume a full field reset.
- **Round end:** the instant a player's life reaches 0 (necessarily via a 3-damage attack hit, since zako floor at 0.5) or a player touches Death. There is **no time-over judgment, no score-out, no sudden death** — Death guarantees eventual resolution.
- **Double KO:** the round is a draw and **both players are credited one round point** (JA Wikipedia). In arcade CPU battles a double KO counts as a **win** for the human. What happens if a double KO would give both players the match-winning point is undocumented (§13).
- **Death (the reaper), the anti-stall enforcer:**
  - Spawns when the round reaches roughly **100 in-game seconds** (observed 110–120 sometimes; the in-game clock runs slower than real time under slowdown; JA Wikipedia says ~102 s). This Death **respawns endlessly** until the round ends.
  - Also spawns early if a player **fires no shots for an extended period** (~30 s per Wikipedia — figure unverified; mikwuyma says the time varies, possibly formation-count based). A Death summoned this way **despawns permanently after one kill**.
  - **Touching Death = instant round loss** ("Death KO"), bypassing all heart rules. Post-hit/bomb i-frames let you pass through him.
  - **HP: 4 on first appearance, +3 per subsequent appearance** (4, 7, 10, 13, …), incrementing whether you destroyed him or he drifted off-screen, **capping at 100 from the 33rd appearance** (503sprites). Killable by shots, chains, or a bomb (bomb = always one hit). Respawn gap ~2–5 seconds ("a couple of seconds" — unmeasured).
  - **Spawn position: mirrored across your field's center line from your ship's X position** at the spawn moment (503sprites).
  - **Movement: continuous pursuit** of the player with a limited turn rate; speed grows over time/appearances up to a hard cap of **256 internal units** (= the Load Ran speed cluster; uniform in all directions, no diagonal bonus). Since players move ~1.4× faster diagonally, **every character can evade Death forever by circling** (Yan's 208 × 1.4 ≈ 294 > 256). At high speed he can be trapped orbiting a stationary player ("satellite rotation"). He persists while off-screen.
  - **Offensive use:** killed Death explodes with **blast power 3** and participates fully in the chain system — detonating formations, catching reverses (and thus feeding boss triggers), shielding. A chain started purely from Death can never award a Perfect (unless a later non-killing shot tags a zako).
  - **REFUTED FOLKLORE:** the claim that Death can be "volleyed to the opponent's side" to instakill them exists only on Wikipedia and its mirrors, uncited, and is contradicted by every expert source (mikwuyma, Mizuumi, 503sprites, NMT, ASaoshi). The documented counterplay around an opponent's Death (timing an EX into their Death-respawn window) would be pointless if transfer existed. **Do not implement Death transfer.** The kernel of truth: each player faces their own Death, and Death's explosion can feed attacks you send.
- **Each player gets their own independent Death instance** (both fields get one at timeout; each tracks its own appearance counter).
- **Arcade (story) mode is NOT round-based:** 7 stages, each a single battle decided by one KO, on a lives/stock system (lose = −1 stock). Continuing wipes the score. Stage clear bonus: 100,000 points per remaining life; top rank requires clearing within 4:30 with 1,000,000+ points (NamuWiki).
- Typical match lengths in practice: 30 seconds to 3 minutes.

### 9.2 Feel notes

- **Fire rate** is governed by the 2-shot on-screen cap: a missed shot locks you out until it exits — fast-shot characters effectively fire faster, and mashing into a whiff can cost you a reflect.
- **Slowdown is a real gameplay variable**: the Neo Geo slows down heavily under load; competitive play (Fightcade, MVS) treats it as part of the game (it acts as a readability/comeback aid during dense volleys, lengthens bomb i-frame real time, and is strategically exploited — baits recommended "during heavy slowdown"). The Dreamcast port's slowdown-free mode demonstrably shortens the bomb invulnerability reaction window. A faithful port must decide whether to simulate it.
- The 1996 developers hand-tuned explosion hitboxes and volley pacing: *"you want it to be like playing beanbag or hackeysack, and timing is key."* Free 8-way movement and dodgeable attacks were explicit non-negotiables.
- Charging is a commitment: you send no attacks while holding, your screen "devolves into mayhem," and you cannot normal-shoot — the bigger the level, the bigger the risk window.
- EX/fireball speed scales up across a match via Perfects (and fever); late-game attacks become "high-speed attack zako impossible to react to or dodge" — the snowball is large and intended.

## 10. RNG, Scoring, Items & Advanced Techniques

### 10.1 What is random vs deterministic

| System | Random | Deterministic |
|---|---|---|
| Formation choice | next formation drawn from a time-expanding pool (Simple-only early); back-attack has a cooldown | the 36 formation paths themselves; **L/R mirroring strictly alternates** |
| Formation internals | a few sub-groups pick a random entry side; orb slot varies among formation-specific slots | per-formation paths, colors, perfect routes |
| Fireball pattern | starting point in the 3-pattern cycle | cycle order (Parabola → Diagonal Bounce → Stop-and-wait); advances on the sender's formation change, even mid-combo |
| Fever orb | which formation it lands in (counter-based, perfect-accelerated, observed formations ~4–12) | only explosions/bombs detonate it |
| Boss behavior | which attack it uses (dev-confirmed random); clockwise/counter mirroring | mode pattern and wander durations per attack; spawn/stun rules |
| Kim EX | detonation time/place | — |
| Mevious EX | loiter wandering, exit direction | — |
| Anomalies | combo occasionally yields an EX instead of fireballs (unexplained); "free" Perfects after cross-formation carryover | — |

### 10.2 Scoring (single-player relevant; included for fidelity)

- **Chain score:** for n ≤ 32 hits, **50·n·(n+1)** points (10-chain = 5,500; 15 = 12,000; 32 = 52,800); for n ≥ 33, **52,800 + 3,200·(n−32)**. Display shows "MAX" from 33 up.
- **Kill-during-display double:** if any player dies while a chain count is still displayed, that chain's points are awarded a second time (works for your own death too).
- **Consecutive-Perfect bonuses:** 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 / 5,000 / 7,000 / 10,000 / 15,000 / 20,000 for Perfects 1–8 — then a famous MVS ROM bug: 9th = 1 pt, 10th = 30,730, 11th = 1, 12th = 30,804, 13th–18th = 2,304 each, 19th = 1, 20th = 515, 21st = 1,028.
- Formation zako: 10–50 pts by durability tier; orb 300 pts; destroyed normal attack 200 pts, reverse 400 pts; dollar-side coin 10 pts. Stage clear: chain bonus (e.g., 26-chain kill = +26,000), 100k per remaining life at the end.
- Arcade continue quirk (low confidence): the arcade resets the score entirely on continue (score always ends in 1 afterward); the DC port offers a fix toggle.

### 10.3 Items (complete list — there is no health item)

| Item | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Bomb coin** | courier zako on a formation-count schedule (first ~50–60 s; second soon after Death) | caught bomb-side-up: +1 bomb ("GET BOMS"); dollar-side-up: 10 pts |
| **Star coin** | replaces an all-bubble formation (after "!!") | caught star-side-up: pops every bubble on your screen; dollar-side: 10 pts |
| **Fever orb** | scheduled into a formation slot (§6.1) | fever mode |
| (P Mark) | Saturn mode / sequel only — not in the arcade game | +1 charge gauge level cap |

All coin-type drops are telegraphed by the same "!!" bubble at the top of the screen.

### 10.4 Perfects

- **Definition:** clear the entire formation while **directly destroying exactly ONE zako** — the chain does the rest. You may shoot (damage) other zako mid-chain as long as your shots don't destroy them. Destroying a second zako yourself voids it.
- **Edge cases:** if the previous formation's lingering explosions pre-destroyed part of the current formation, clearing the remainder from one explosion still counts; Perfects can even register **with no shot fired** (cross-formation carryover; mechanism unexplained). Killing Death as the combo starter voids the Perfect unless a later non-killing shot tags a zako. Higher rank (1P mode) widens the leniency window.
- **Rewards:** a **fixed** charge-meter chunk (independent of combo size); the fever orb scheduled into an earlier formation; escalating score (§10.2); and a **cumulative tempo shift** — your outgoing attacks get faster, incoming attacks slower, and formation pacing shifts. ⚠ Direction conflict on the formation half: Mizuumi says a Perfect "slows formations on YOUR screen and speeds up the OPPONENT's"; mikwuyma says your own offense gets slightly faster with the opponent-slow part "unconfirmed"; ZillionBrain (most precise) says your attacks speed up, opponent's attacks slow, **and your own next formation spawns sooner**. Canonical reading: every effect favors the Perfect-getter; attack-travel speed (yours up, theirs down) is solid; the formation-spawn direction needs emulator verification (§13). No numeric magnitudes are published.

### 10.5 Advanced techniques (documented high-level play)

- **Dodge normals, reflect reverses** — the foundational rule (§4).
- **Cluster counting:** before chain-reflecting a mixed cluster, ensure it holds fewer than three of the opponent's NORMAL fireballs, or you'll feed them a boss.
- **Baiting aimed objects:** stand where you want fireballs/aimed EXs to lock, move late ("you generally want to move later rather than earlier"). Homing pigbirds: bait with a sharp direction reversal.
- **Charge-while-chaining:** hold charge while your chain continues; holding to MAX is a high-risk panic/payoff tool since you emit nothing while charging.
- **High-speed LV2 (高速ＬＶ２):** spend Lv2 when accumulated Perfects make your EXs fast; stack Lv2/MAX over an active boss for overlapping pressure.
- **Mass-reflect with charges:** charge attacks reflect fireballs like shots do — a charge sweep through a reverse cluster can trigger the 3-reverse boss.
- **Death control:** pre-damage Death to 1 HP (shot-counting is reliable to ~his 5th appearance) and pop him on demand as a chain detonator/shield, or to buy his respawn gap during a dangerous wave; "satellite rotation" to neutralize a max-speed Death; attack into the opponent's Death-respawn window (死神ＬＶ２).
- **Doping (vs CPU/score play):** deliberately taking zako hits to heal a near-dead opponent (+0.5/+1.0) and keep a round alive for scoring.
- **Orb skipping (オーブ見逃し):** deliberately letting fever orbs/formations pass on score stages.
- **Chain reignition:** hold rapid fire near the top of the screen to ignite the next spawning formation while the chain counter is still up.
- **Boss handling:** aim only at the boss's center; let a damaged boss escape rather than killing it when the kill reward isn't needed; bomb a boss precisely as it emits specials to cancel them; exploit the spawn-frame no-stun for double heavy charges.

The Mizuumi infobox states it outright, and all organized tournaments (2017–present) ran on Fightcade.

## 12. Story-Mode AI Behavior Notes

- **Structure:** 7 stages, one single-KO battle each, stock-based (§7). Opponents: 4 randomized from the cast, then fixed **Dark Ran → Mevious → Queen Memory**; Load Ran transforms into **Sprites** for the final battle. 8 operator difficulty levels (1–8); a Level-8 1CC signals solid fundamentals but is well below human-vs-human depth.
- **Dodging AI:** "uncannily good at dodging your attacks" (HG101). The 2018 Matsushita interview reveals it was the project's hardest problem — humanlike dodging built with **fuzzy logic**, including trained circular dodging; the director split his credit with the programmer over it.
- **Exploitable quirks:** the FIRST opponent often loses all dodging comprehension and **stops shooting entirely once the player exceeds ~100,000 points** (score-break, shmups forum); early-stage AI degrades while later AI stays sharp.
- **Rank:** single-player uses a hidden rank value that scales formation movement speed dramatically (high rank visibly changes formation geometry — some movements are time-parameterized, so speed scaling alters where groups converge) and widens/narrows Perfect timing windows.
- **Score-play interactions:** the CPU can be kept alive via doping (§10.5); JP guides recommend letting nearly all formations pass on stages 6–7 while farming; the CPU's life obeys the identical lifesteal economy (the entire NMT doping arithmetic was measured against the CPU).
- **Mechanics parity:** all sources treat the CPU battles as the same versus engine (same damage, ladder, fever, Death); no exceptions documented, but it is assumed rather than proven (§13).
- Mevious's pre-fight dialogue tracks how many continues you've used (4th-wall gag); a double KO vs the CPU counts as a player win.

## 13. UNCERTAINTIES

Honest list of everything refuted, contradicted, single-sourced-and-shaky, or unverifiable. Spot-check items marked ⚙ in real gameplay/emulation before locking implementation.

**Refuted / corrected during verification (do not implement the original claim):**

1. ~~"All interaction between players happens via attack objects"~~ — false; life transfer, Perfect tempo effects, and boss clash/displacement are non-object channels.
2. ~~"Fever roughly triples output"~~ — it "more than doubles" (~2× long-chain asymptote, ~2.67× at 9 hits).
3. ~~"Each reverse reflected = exactly 1 extra, no limit, unconditionally"~~ — 3+ reverses destroyed in one combo's explosions become a **boss instead** of extras.
4. ~~Defensive cluster rule "check fewer than 3 REVERSE attacks"~~ — inverted; the source says fewer than three **NORMAL** attacks.
5. ~~"Schmitt's EX is an aimed rocket"~~ — it spawns at the bottom and flies straight up; never aimed.

7. ~~"Boss transfer + extra-attack flood happens whenever a new boss is triggered with one on screen"~~ — that wording is scoped to the simultaneous-double-trigger case; the single-trigger reversal spawns YOUR character's boss with no documented flood.

10. ~~"Boss center-only damage is absolute"~~ — exception: Mevious's boss tail attack moves the hurtbox to the tail tip.
